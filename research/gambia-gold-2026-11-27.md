# Buying gold in The Gambia (2026-11-27)

Goal: work out what gold costs in The Gambia, what it sells for, and how and where to buy it.
Nothing here is financial or legal advice. Check the legal points with the Gambian Ministry and HMRC before acting.

## 2. What we can buy it for

- **Jewellery (goldsmiths, Albert Market in Banjul, Serrekunda market):** expect the melt value above plus a making
  charge. No published dealer prices exist. Haggling is normal. Estimate: melt value plus 8 to 15% (this range is
  our assumption, not a quoted figure).
- **Raw gold, dust or "bars" from brokers:** the real price is spot minus only a small discount (a few percent) for
  assay and refining. **An offer far below spot (10 to 30% off) is the classic sign of a scam.** Nobody
  sells real gold well below what any refinery would pay them.

## 3. What we can sell it for

- **In the UK:** bullion dealers and refiners pay below spot for unbranded gold or jewellery, after an assay. Estimate:
  85 to 92% of melt value (our assumption; get quotes). Gold without paperwork showing where it came from triggers
  anti-money-laundering checks, and some buyers will refuse it.
- **Round trip on 100 g of 22K jewellery:** melt value £8,223. Bought for about £8,880 to £9,460, it sells for about £6,990 to £7,560.
  **Expected loss: 15 to 26%**, before flights, insurance and any duty.
- **Conclusion: there is no price gap between The Gambia and the UK to profit from.** Gold is priced worldwide. A cheap
  price in The Gambia means fake gold, stolen gold or smuggled gold.

## 4. The law (The Gambia)

- Dealing in gold is regulated under the **Mines and Quarries Act 2005**. The regulator is the Geological Department of the
  Ministry of Petroleum, Energy and Mines (MoPE). **Dealing in gold without a licence is illegal.** Goldsmiths do not get
  dealing licences.
- In a criminal trial reported around 2014, the department testified that only one company had been licensed to deal in gold. By 2025,
  more licences existed: Wuria Ventures got a "special licence to possess and deal in gold" on 2025-11-28.
- We found no published procedure for export permits. **Ask the Geological Department in writing before buying anything you
  plan to take out of the country.**
- The Gambia mines almost no gold of its own. Gold traded there mostly comes from small-scale and illegal mining in
  eastern Senegal, Mali and Guinea. That supply chain is tied to smuggling routes to Dubai and, in Mali, to conflict.
  Buying from it carries risks of seizure and of breaking money-laundering laws.

## 5. Fraud: the main risk

- **2025/26 case:** a Dubai firm (Arabia Gulf Diamonds DMCC) paid about **$3.6M** through *licensed* Gambian brokers (Wuria
  Ventures, B & M Gold & Silver Brokerage). KK Gold Refinery (Bijilo) certified the bars at 96 to 97% pure. Police tests found
  **2 to 5% gold**. A civil suit is in the High Court, and these are allegations, not rulings. **A licence and a certificate
  from the seller's own refinery prove nothing.**
- Common tricks: a real sample followed by fake bulk gold, "export fees" demanded in advance, swapped bags, invitations to a
  village or a mine, and "customs is holding the shipment, pay to release it".
- The US State Department and the UK government both warn that financial scams are common in The Gambia.

## 6. How we could do it (only safe ways)

1. **Small, personal amounts:** buy hallmarked jewellery from an established shop. Get a receipt with the weight and
   purity. Test it with an XRF machine (an independent jeweller can do this). Declare it at customs on both sides. Treat it as
   jewellery, not as an investment.
2. **Investment:** buy in the UK instead. Investment gold is VAT-free, Britannias and Sovereigns are free of capital gains tax,
   and premiums are a few percent. This beats any Gambian route on cost and safety.
3. **A real trade in Gambian gold (a business):** this needs a MoPE licence, an export permit, an independent assay
   (SGS, Bureau Veritas or similar) **before any money moves**, an escrow or letter of credit paid only on delivery to a
   refinery, and due diligence on sources to OECD/LBMA standards. This is a regulated commodity business, not a trade.

## 7. UK customs

gov.uk's cash page lists gold coins, bullion and nuggets as declarable at €10,000 or more for Northern Ireland. For Great Britain, the
official list (£10,000 or more) didn't clearly include gold in what we found. **Declare it anyway, and confirm with HMRC before
travelling.** Undeclared gold can be seized.
