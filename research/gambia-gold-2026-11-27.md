# Buying gold in The Gambia (2026-11-27)

Goal: what gold costs in The Gambia, what it sells for, and how and where to buy it.
Nothing here is financial or legal advice. The legal points need confirming with the Gambian Ministry and HMRC (questions at the end).
Research method: a first pass by hand, then three research agents (where to buy, law and export, sell side). Many Gambian and gov.uk pages
were blocked to the agents, so some quotes come from search snippets. These are marked *(snippet)*.

**There is no public register of licensed dealers, and no independent gold assay lab in The Gambia.** The Gambia Standards Bureau
only does calibration. The nearest accredited gold lab is SGS Senegal, which serves mining companies.

## 3. What we can buy it for

- **Jewellery:** melt value plus a making charge. No published figures exist; we assume 8–15%. Collect real quotes in person.
- **Raw gold, dust or bars:** a real seller asks spot minus a few percent at most. **Anything 10–30% below spot is the scam
  signature.** For a non-licensee, buying raw gold is also illegal (see section 5).

Without the import tax (at or under the £390 allowance, so tiny amounts only) the loss is still **17–30%**. Avoiding a declaration
on larger amounts would be evasion, and is not an option. **Conclusion: there is no price gap between The Gambia and the UK to profit from.**

## 5. The law: The Gambia

- **Mines and Quarries Act 2005** is still the main statute; no later mining act was found. Gold is a **"restricted mineral"**
  (with silver, diamond and platinum).
- **Restricted Minerals Regulations 2018** *(snippet)*: "A Special licence is required to deal in restricted minerals"; **"no person
  shall purchase any Restricted Minerals unless he is the holder of a Special licence"**; "deal in" = "purchase, sell, receive or
  dispose of". A licence lasts 1 year and needs a registered Gambian business, fixed premises and proof of funds. There are penalties for dealing without one.
  - **So a foreigner buying raw gold, dust or bars without a licence is breaking the law as a buyer, not only the seller.**
  - Whether finished jewellery counts as a "restricted mineral" is **not confirmed**. Ask the Ministry.
- **Export:** an older Minerals Act says "No person shall export any restricted mineral from The Gambia unless he has obtained a
  licence from the Minister". This is not confirmed as current. No export duty, royalty or fee schedule was found. Personal exports need customs clearance.
- **Anti-money laundering:** the AML/CFT Act 2012 and FIU guidelines for non-financial businesses (2016). The dealer cash threshold in dalasi was not found
  (the FATF international benchmark is $/€15,000).
- **Context:** The Gambia mines almost no gold of its own. Supply comes from small-scale and illegal mining in Senegal, Mali and Guinea, routes
  linked to smuggling to Dubai. Interpol's Operation Sanu targeted illegal mining in The Gambia in 2024.

## 6. The law: UK

- **Cash declaration:** for **Great Britain**, gold is *not* on the list of "cash" to declare (£10,000+). It is on the list for **Northern Ireland**.
- **But:** jewellery above the **£390** allowance must be declared as goods. Expect 20% import VAT and a 2% duty rate (possibly 0% for
  Gambian-origin goods under the developing-countries scheme; unverified for travellers). **Investment gold** imports are VAT-exempt (VAT Notice
  701/21, which mentions an import code, CPC 40 00 073).
- **Seizure:** under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, gold "whether manufactured or not" is a "listed asset" (s.303B). Officers can seize it on
  reasonable suspicion (s.303J). Gold with no receipts or export papers is exposed to this.
- **Tax on UK-bought gold:** investment gold has no VAT, and Britannias and Sovereigns are free of capital gains tax. Premiums: 1 kg bar ~1–2.6% over spot.

## 7. Fraud patterns to expect

- **The 2026 Gambian case:** $3.6M (and a separate or overlapping $1.1M claim) paid to *licensed* brokers for 214 kg of "doré". It was certified
  96–97% by the seller-linked refinery and tested at 1–5%. **A licence plus a local certificate proves nothing.**
- Advance "export permit / tax / customs release" fees: the US government calls these "very likely a scam".
- A real sample shown, then fakes delivered; a test you "witness" that has been rigged.
- **Tungsten-core bars** pass weight, magnet and surface XRF tests. Only ultrasound, electromagnetic or fire assay catches them.
- **Protection:** pay no fees up front, never rely on a local refinery's certificate, and pay only after an independent fire assay, ideally on
  delivery to a UK assay office or refiner.

## 8. Options

1. **Owning gold as an investment:** buy in the UK (VAT-free; Britannias and Sovereigns free of capital gains tax; ~1–5% premium; sells back at ~95–97%).
   This is better than any Gambian route on cost, legality and safety.
2. **A personal piece of Gambian jewellery:** buy from an established goldsmith with a receipt showing weight and purity, have it tested
   in the UK, and declare it above £390. Treat it as a purchase, not an investment (expect a 30–43% loss if resold).
3. **A gold business out of The Gambia:** needs a Special Licence (Gambian company, premises, funds), an export licence, an independent
   fire assay before any money moves, escrow paid on delivery to an LBMA refiner, and OECD-standard checks on where the gold came from. There is no
   price gap to fund this. Any profit would have to come from being a properly licensed exporter close to the miners, which is a regulated
   commodity business with high fraud and compliance risk.

## Questions only the authorities can answer

- **Geological Department (MoPE):** Does a tourist need a licence or an export certificate to buy and take out gold *jewellery*? Is there an
  export licence today, with what fees and royalty? Is there a register of licensed dealers? What is the status of the Wuria and B & M licences?
- **Gambia Revenue Authority customs:** Any declaration requirement or limit for jewellery leaving by air?
- **Gambian FIU:** What is the dealer cash threshold?
- **HMRC:** Confirm gold is not "cash" for Great Britain arrivals. Does the 0% developing-country duty rate apply to personal imports of Gambian jewellery? What proof of
  origin or purity is accepted?
